## Main finding — the original TASK-102 status boundary was lost

TASK-102 already removed superseded failures from the *operator's current*
`Failed executions` view using a read-only, published first-parent **ticket
lifecycle** proof (a deletion of the original workflow path), independent of
product lineage. TASK-137 subsequently reused that helper for the distinct
question of authorizing **new worker generations** and added global
`worker_identity`, product HEAD, retry, recovery and same-blob invariants.
That conflation revived TASK-088 whenever unrelated TASK-151 was running.

TASK-153 fixes the specific foreign-worker case but retains the underlying
coupling: `_failure_superseded(..., for_status=True)` still refuses to hide a
proved-done historical failure when **unrelated product work** leaves the
product checkout dirty, locally ahead, not yet fetched, or otherwise mismatched
with remote. Those observations can and must restrict scheduler admission,
but cannot by themselves make TASK-088's completed lifecycle current again.

The new `_ticket_supersession_proof()` also runs `git rev-list` for full
first-parent history and launches one `git ls-tree` subprocess for **every**
commit between failure and current control HEAD, including commits unrelated
to the ticket. That expensive proof existed in TASK-137, not TASK-102; the
new process-local cache only hides repeated cost at an unchanged HEAD and
does not justify retaining it in this status fix.

## Required correction — narrow, not a new framework

**Primary task:** restore the independent, previously functioning TASK-102
projection for historical failure visibility, while keeping TASK-137's strict
admission/retry policy intact.

1. For the `status` current-failure list, prove the *old failure's own*
   published first-parent workflow transition/removal on a clean, authoritative
   control checkout. Check only matching ticket/execution ownership, recovery
   and disposal markers before suppressing. A worker, ref retirement, or
   product branch mutation belonging to another ticket must not revive it.
   Fail closed on unreadable/unpublished/divergent control or unresolved
   matching generation. Never delete or rewrite stored failure/report history.
2. Do **not** require product HEAD equality, clean product tree or product
   ancestry to decide whether a previously superseded failure should be
   *displayed*. Retain those strict conditions as needed when deciding new
   worker admission/retry and report distinct blockers in `plan`.
3. Prefer restoring TASK-102's efficient, narrowly scoped
   `git log --first-parent ... --diff-filter=D -- <ticket workflow paths>`
   lifecycle observation (or an equivalently bounded event-based query).
   Do not scan every intervening commit with `ls-tree` from the status path.
   Explicitly handle same-name deletion/recreation, changed ticket content
   and ambiguous history conservatively: an unrelated file deletion is
   insufficient; do not suppress a genuinely new failure generation.
4. Remove the current `for_status` policy switch and the duplicated
   status/admission evaluation whenever a small separation of responsibility
   is simpler. One projection helper may interpret lifecycle events, and
   the existing admission helper may keep the stricter TASK-137 proof.
   Share **low-level Git observation only where genuinely DRY**; do not build
   a generic policy engine, new persistent state or an extensive abstraction.
5. Audit heterogeneous state markers (some use `ticket_id`, others a
   branch/execution identity). An unrelated marker must not be treated as
   the current ticket merely because `ticket_id` is missing. Unknown
   ownership remains fail-closed where it really is uncertain.

## Focused acceptance and regression tests

  versus TASK-151 (different running worker) situation in disposable real Git;
  current failure absent in human/JSON status, original history preserved.
- Keep that old failure absent while unrelated product HEAD moves ahead,
  product checkout is dirty or product local/remote differs. A new worker
  may be blocked for separate valid admission reasons.
- An unresolved matching worker/recovery/manual retry or genuinely **new**
  failed execution for that ticket remains visible and does not gain
  unauthorized scheduler/retry admission.
- Proved deferral/requeue, deletion, later same-ticket edits and
  deletion/recreation are distinguished; ambiguous or unpublished control
  does not falsely retire failures.
- One lightweight instrumented Git-command-count regression for hundreds
  of unrelated control commits and very few ticket changes; no per-commit
  `ls-tree` subprocess storm. Reuse existing fixtures and tests instead of
  proliferating private-helper permutations.
- Preserve TASK-137/TASK-138 safety regressions, current first-party
  owner/WAL/CAS behavior, full configured tests and exact-checkpoint CI.

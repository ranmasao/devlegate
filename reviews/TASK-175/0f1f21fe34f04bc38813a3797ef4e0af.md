## P0 — not the requested integrated status presentation

The canonical TASK-175 specifically rejects `simply adding an isolated plan table` and requires the scheduler decision to be integrated *organically* with status/execution/lifecycle and workflow. The implementation at `cli.py:_render_status_text`, immediately after rendering `Active executions`, unconditionally constructs `scheduler_rows` and appends:

```python
lines.extend(["", render_table("Scheduler decision (observed)", scheduler_rows)])
```

This is the exact disallowed layout, a stand-alone plan report appended to existing status. It separately prints `Action`, `Reason`, `Ticket`, and `Bound resume`, then appends another sentence saying that service stop/restart or live execution takes precedence. In active cases the ticket cell says `see active execution above`; in other cases it says `see blocked tickets below`, `see review tickets below`, or `see accepted tickets below`. These are cross-references that require the operator to reconcile several separate representations instead of presenting one coherent state. For a stopped runnable service, the report prints `run-worker` followed by a later disclaimer that no launch is scheduled. The tests assert `"Scheduler decision (observed)" in output`, thereby codifying the incorrect layout.

The actual desired solution is **not** to rename the table, merge the labels into another table, or append a new standalone `Next` report. Convey status/precedence, active execution or recovery, and selected next action *in the existing hierarchy as one logical account*. For active execution prioritize the evidence-backed execution information and embed the planner's relevant explanatory reason without a second duplicate ticket. For stopping/restarting, make lifecycle intent the primary status and qualify subordinate planner intent where meaningful. For stopped idle/runnable, say what the observed scheduler would do **if service were started**, rather than display a seemingly live next admission and retract it later. Blocked/review/accepted reasons must relate directly to their workflow sections without opaque `see ... above/below` text. Exact wording/layout remains implementer-owned; the key acceptance requirement is semantic coherence and avoiding an independent plan table.
